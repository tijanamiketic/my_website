Once you know what each LLM is for and how to use it well (see [“Differences between LLMs”](differences-between-llms)), the next step is learning how to actually work with it. To start, a handful of key rules is all you need.

## Key rules

1. **Use English as your base.** For most LLMs, English is their “native” language, so it's what they understand best. If English doesn't come easily to you, work on it — an LLM can help with that too. Until then, talk in your own language, translate the conclusions, and continue the conversation in English in a new chat. The goal is to gradually get comfortable in English.
2. **Give it a role that fits your goal.** If the point of the session is to learn something, treat it as a teacher; if you need help with work, treat it as a collaborator you delegate tasks to. Spell the role out in the prompt itself (“Act as a teacher…”) — it will step into it more fully.
3. **You make the decisions.** However much you learn from an LLM, the decisions are still yours. Use it to feed your knowledge, creativity and inspiration, but don't let it decide for you — it gives you information, you choose.
4. **Personalise the settings.** The more precisely you describe yourself and your goals in the settings, the better it will understand you.
5. **Be clear.** Don't expect it to guess what you want if you haven't explained it. If you don't know yet yourself, use one session to figure out what you need, and ask for it clearly in the next.
6. **Give context and an example.** Tell it who the answer is for, how long it should be and what form it should take (a list, a table, an email). If you have an example of the result you want, include it.
7. **Check the answers.** An LLM can sound confident even when it's wrong or making things up. Always verify important facts, numbers and sources somewhere reliable.

## Example: from a weak prompt to a better one

**Weak prompt:** “Write something about healthy eating.”

**Better prompt:** *“You are a nutritionist. Write a one-week meal plan for a busy student on a tight budget, as a table with breakfast, lunch and dinner.”*

**Weak prompt:** “Explain Excel to me.”

**Better prompt:** *“Act as my teacher. I'm a complete beginner in Excel. Explain the 5 functions I'll use most, with one simple example each, and end with a short exercise.”*

The better prompt gives a role, context, a goal and the shape of the answer — and it's written in English.

## To wrap up

An LLM is only as good as your sense of what you're asking it for. Write in English when you can, give it a role that matches your goal, introduce yourself through the settings, be precise in your requests — and always keep the decisions for yourself.

Don't expect a perfect answer on the first try. Prompting is a skill you learn by practising: ask, look at the result, rephrase and ask again. Over time you'll get faster at steering it, and the LLM will become an assistant that saves you time and broadens your horizons — not a replacement for your own thinking.
