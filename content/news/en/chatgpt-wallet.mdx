On Friday, November 13, OpenAI launched **ChatGPT Wallet**, a wallet that lets its AI agents complete purchases on the user's behalf. Until now, an agent could research products and fill the cart, but paying was left to the user. Now it can go one step further.

## What "dots" are and how Wallet works

At the end of September, OpenAI introduced **dots**, personal agents that run constantly in the background, connect to thousands of apps and handle tasks for the user. Wallet is the piece they were missing: a way to pay safely.

Here's how it works:

1. The user saves a card in Wallet once.
2. They ask their agent to find and buy something.
3. Before every purchase, they **approve it personally** with a biometric check, such as Face ID or a fingerprint.
4. The card network creates a **token** for that purchase, a stand-in number. Neither the agent nor the merchant ever sees the real card number.

Wallet was built in partnership with **Visa, Mastercard and American Express**. Card benefits and protections, such as rewards points and purchase protection, still apply.

## Who can use it

The launch is deliberately narrow:

- only **ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US**, aged 18 or older;
- only eligible **credit cards** issued in the US (debit and virtual cards aren't supported for now);
- it works only inside "dots" agents, not in regular chat.

OpenAI says the limited release is meant to test how the system works with real merchants and banks before a wider rollout. There's no date yet for Europe or Serbia.

## Why this is a big step

Researching products is easy, and AI already does it well. Completing the purchase in the same conversation is harder, but much more useful. If the experiment succeeds, it changes how we shop, make bookings and use everyday services.

OpenAI is also announcing the next phase: users will be able to set a **spending limit and rules**, and the agent will be able to buy within them without approving every single purchase.

## Risks not to overlook

- **Mistakes:** what if the agent buys the wrong thing? Who refunds the money, and how? There's no clear answer yet.
- **Fraud and too much authority:** the more freedom an agent has, the greater the damage when something goes wrong.
- **Merchants:** some stores block automated shoppers, so the agent won't be able to pay everywhere.

That's why OpenAI is starting cautiously, and with good reason. Still, the direction is clear: agents that research, decide and act, with human oversight, are moving from demos into real products.
